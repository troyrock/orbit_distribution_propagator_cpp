What the sliders show

At simulated orbital nodes, the surface shows measured Monte Carlo times. Between nodes, it interpolates log time in altitude, inclination, and eccentricity; it does not run new trajectories.

Model, interpolation, and limitations

The plot connects the measured position and velocity uncertainty nodes to display a surface; its uncertainty axes are logarithmic. Continuous orbital sliders use trilinear interpolation of the logarithm of coverage time between up to eight neighboring simulated orbits. A value is shown only if every contributing corner has valid coverage. Missing or invalid corner results leave gaps instead of being filled. A gap at an interpolated orbit means a required corner is unavailable; it does not establish that the selected orbit's own uncertain cloud intersects Earth.

Hover brackets describe the sampled onset interval at a measured node. At interpolated orbits, their endpoints are interpolated by the same rule; they are not guaranteed event bounds. Neither kind of bracket is a Monte Carlo confidence interval. Coverage means the configured finite-sample phase criterion is met, not uniform probability around the orbit.

Perigee is the nominal orbit's lowest altitude; individual uncertain samples can have lower perigees. Tail counts report initial sample perigees below 300 km and 500 km in the contributing ensembles. The gravity-only model omits atmospheric drag, so those samples require care. Tail counts are not averaged into invented fractional particles.
